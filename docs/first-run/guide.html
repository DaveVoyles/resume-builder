Your job search, one page

You talk. A helper on your computer does the filing, the writing, and the checking.

You finish with three things: a resume that only says what you really did, a list of the jobs you are chasing, and a short guide to read the night before an interview.

  1. 1

    See the map

    You: look at this page. Ask what any step means.

    You get: the whole path, before any of your real history is used.

  2. 2

    Hand over old material

    You: give your helper old resumes, notes, or a link to work you have already published.

    You get: a private folder on your computer. It is not published.

  3. 3

    Answer one question at a time

    You: correct anything that is vague or untrue. Skip a question if you do not know.

    You get: a record of your real work, in your words.

  4. 4

    Pick one job

    You: paste a job link, or say which role you want.

    You get: a check that the link still works, and a yes or no from you before any resume is written.

  5. 5

    Read the resume

    You: open the Word file and mark anything you would not say out loud.

    You get: a resume aimed at that one job. It is not sent anywhere.

  6. 6

    Watch the list

    You: glance at one page that lists every job you are chasing.

    You get: status in plain words: not yet, applied, interview, offer, or no.

  7. 7

    Say what happened

    You: tell your helper “I applied,” “I have an interview,” or “they said no.”

    You get: the list updated. You do not edit a spreadsheet.

  8. 8

    Prepare for the conversation

    You: ask for a study guide before the interview.

    You get: talking points tied to work you actually did, plus the gaps you should not pretend to have.

What you never have to do

  • Write code, or learn a new tool.
  • Send an application from this page. You still choose when anything is submitted.
  • Let a sentence stay on the resume if it is not true. Say so, and it comes off.
