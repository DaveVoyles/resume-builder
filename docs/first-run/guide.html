How this job search works

You talk. A helper on your computer does the filing and the writing. This page is the whole trip, in order. Nothing here is sent to an employer.

A made-up example, so the steps feel real

Meet Alex Rivera. Alex is not you. Alex wants a Senior Product Manager job at a made-up company called Northwind Tools. Along the way Alex has two things in hand: an old resume, and one job posting.

An old resume

Alex Rivera

Product manager, Contoso Labs

2022 to 2024

Led the launch of an internal tool for one product team.

A job to chase

Senior Product Manager

Northwind Tools

They want someone who has shipped a product with a team.

  1. 1

    Read this page

    You are doing this step right now. Ask your helper what any step means. You do not need to hand over your own history yet.

    For Alex, this is just getting oriented. No resume has been written.

  2. 2

    Share something you already have

    An old resume, a notes file, or a link to work you already published is enough. Your helper puts it in a private folder on your computer. That folder is not published.

    Alex drops in the Contoso Labs resume from 2022, plus a short note: “The tool was for one team, not the whole company.”

  3. 3

    Answer one question at a time

    Your helper will ask about jobs you have had, the kind of role you want, and where you can work. Correct anything that is wrong. Skip a question if you do not know.

    Alex is asked, “What did you do at Contoso Labs?” Alex says, “I led one product launch. I did not manage a budget.”

  4. 4

    Pick one job

    Paste a link, or say the company and the job title. Your helper checks that the link still opens. You say yes before any resume is written.

    Alex pastes the Northwind Tools posting for Senior Product Manager. The link still works. Alex says, “Yes, write one for this.”

  5. 5

    Read the new resume

    You get a Word file aimed at that one job. Read it. Say so if a sentence is something you would not say out loud. It comes off. Nothing is emailed.

    The draft says, “Led a launch used by several teams.” Alex says, “Change that. It was one team.” The helper changes it.

  6. 6

    Look at your job list

    One page lists every job you are chasing. Each row says where things stand: not yet, applied, interview, offer, or no.

    Alex’s list has one row: Northwind Tools, Senior Product Manager, Not yet.

  7. 7

    Tell your helper what happened

    When you apply, get an interview, or hear a no, just say that. Your helper updates the list. You do not keep a spreadsheet.

    A week later Alex says, “I applied on Tuesday.” The row changes from Not yet to Applied.

  8. 8

    Get ready for the interview

    Ask for a short guide the night before. It uses work you really did. It also says what you have not done, so you do not have to pretend.

    Alex’s guide says how to talk about the Contoso launch, and it says plainly that Alex has not managed a budget.

A few kind rules

  • You never have to write code.
  • This page does not apply to jobs for you. You still choose when anything is sent.
  • If a sentence on the resume is not true, say so. It comes off.
  • Alex, Contoso Labs, and Northwind Tools are made up. Your helper will use your real story instead.
