One resume for one job.

You talk, and your helper writes that resume, keeps a list of the job, and can write a short guide the night before an interview.

You say when a sentence is wrong, and nothing is sent until you say so.

  1. 1

    Look around this page

    You get the whole trip before you share any work history.

    Your helper opened this page so you can see where this goes. You do not hand over your history yet. Read the steps. Open any box. When you want to begin, tell your helper you are ready.

    You could say: "I'm ready. I have an old resume."

  2. 2

    Hand over an old resume or a few notes

    Your story stays in a private place on your computer.

    Show this stepHide this step

    An old resume is enough. A notes file is enough. A link to work you already published is enough. Your helper puts it in a folder on your computer. That folder is not published.

    You could say: "Here is my old resume, and a note about what that job really was."

  3. 3

    Answer one question at a time

    You get a story you would say out loud. Skip any question you do not know.

    Show this stepHide this step

    Your helper asks about jobs you have had, the kind of role you want, and where you can work. Correct anything that is wrong the moment you hear it. If you do not know, say skip.

    Sample question: "What did you do at Contoso Labs?" Alex says: "I led one product launch. I did not manage a budget."

    Sample, made up. Alex Rivera at Contoso Labs.

  4. 4

    Pick one job

    The resume waits until you say yes.

    Show this stepHide this step

    Paste a link, or say the company and the job title. Your helper checks that the link still opens. If the posting is gone, you pick another. Your helper does not write a resume until you say yes.

    Alex says: "Yes, write one for this."

    Sample, made up. Northwind Tools.

    Northwind Tools

    Senior Product Manager. The link still opens.

  5. 5

    Read the new resume

    You get a copy you can open, written for that one job, and nothing is emailed.

    Show this stepHide this step

    Read each line as if you were saying it out loud. Your helper wrote it from what you shared and from the one job you picked. If a line is wrong, say so. Your helper takes it off. Nothing is emailed. Nothing is sent until you say so.

    You could say: "Change that. It was one team."

    Sample, made up. Alex Rivera, reading a resume for Northwind Tools.

    Alex Rivera

    Senior product manager

    Led a launch used by several teams.

    Led a launch used by one team.

  6. 6

    Look at your list

    You get one page that shows where that job stands.

    Show this stepHide this step

    One page lists every job you are chasing. Each row says where things stand: not yet, applied, interview, offer, or no. You can open it any time. Your helper keeps it current.

    You could say: "Show me the list."

    Sample, made up

    Northwind Tools

    Senior Product Manager. Not yet.

  7. 7

    Tell your helper what happened

    The list updates. Saying it does not send an application.

    Show this stepHide this step

    When you apply, get an interview, or hear a no, just say that. Your helper updates the list. You do not keep a second list of your own. You still choose when anything is sent.

    Alex says: "I applied on Tuesday." The row changes from Not yet to Applied.

    Sample, made up.

  8. 8

    Ask for a short guide the night before

    It uses work you really did, and it names what you have not done.

    Show this stepHide this step

    Ask your helper for a short guide. It is for that one conversation. It uses the launch you really led. It also says what you have not done, so you do not have to pretend.

    You could say: "Write the short guide for tomorrow."

    Sample, made up. Alex Rivera.

    Say this

    How Alex led the Contoso launch for one team.

    Leave this out

    Alex has not managed a budget.
